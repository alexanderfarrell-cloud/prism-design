Payroll Scheduling & Calendar Logic — Explained

A short primer for developers: what a pay schedule is, the key dates that drive it, and how the logic fits together.

What is a Pay Schedule?

A pay schedule is the configuration that defines when and how often employees get paid. It's the foundation for every payroll run. The schedule answers: What dates does this pay period cover? When is payday? When must payroll be submitted so paychecks arrive on time?

Construction companies often run different schedules for different groups—e.g., weekly for field crews (common in the industry) and bi-weekly for office staff. Each schedule is configured separately.

Key Dates — Summary

Date Definition Derived From
Period start First day of the pay period Work week start + frequency + first period anchor
Period end Last day of the pay period Period start + frequency (7, 14, or month days)
Check date (payday) When employees receive pay User-selected; must be ≥ period end + ACH lead time
Payroll deadline When payroll must be submitted Check date − ACH lead time (business days)

The first period start and first check date are the anchor. All future periods are projected from these two. Change the anchor, and the entire calendar recalculates.

How the Logic Comes Together

  1. Work week start — Defines the 7-day cycle for FLSA overtime. Overtime is calculated per work week; a misconfigured start day corrupts every overtime calc downstream.
  2. Pay frequency — Weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly, or monthly. Determines how period start/end dates are computed.
  3. First period + first check date — The anchor. All future periods are projected from these two dates.
  4. Payment speed (ACH lead time) — 2, 4, or 7 business days. Drives the payroll deadline: deadline = check date − lead time. If the check date is too soon after period end, the system blocks it (the "Friday Crunch" guardrail).
