## Gusto: Competitor Teardown

### Why Gusto Matters

Gusto is a different kind of threat than QuickBooks. Where QBO is the entrenched incumbent your users already have, Gusto is the **UX benchmark** — the product that has taught SMB owners what simple payroll *should feel like.* With 400,000+ direct SMB customers, $500M+ in annual revenue, and consistently top-rated UX (4.6/5 across 4,000+ reviews), Gusto sets the expectation for ease-of-use that your product will be measured against.

The strategic question isn't "can Gusto handle construction payroll?" (it can't, not really). It's: **"can we match Gusto's simplicity while delivering construction-grade depth?"**

### Pricing Breakdown

| Tier | Base/month | Per employee/month | Key adds over prior tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple | $49–54 | $6–7 | Full-service payroll, auto tax filing, unlimited runs |
| Plus | $60–89 | $9–13 | Multi-state payroll, time tracking, PTO management, hiring tools |
| Premium | $199 | $24 | Dedicated CSM, HR resource center, compliance alerts, certified HR experts |
| Contractor Only | $35 | $6/contractor | 1099 payments only, stripped-down features |

For a 15-person crew: roughly **$139–$549/month** depending on tier. Most construction SMBs would need Plus for multi-state support, landing around **$195–$284/month**.

### What Gusto Does Well (Your UX Target)

Gusto's core strength isn't a feature — it's a design philosophy. The things worth studying:

**Guided payroll runs.** Running payroll feels like a checkout flow, not an ERP operation. Review hours → confirm amounts → approve → done. Each step is visually clear with inline validation. This is the interaction pattern your "Complexity Abstraction" needs to match or exceed.

**Onboarding as product.** Employee self-onboarding (tax forms, direct deposit, benefits enrollment) is handled through a consumer-grade flow. The employer barely touches it. For construction, imagine: new hire gets a link, fills in their info on their phone from the job site, and they're payroll-ready.

**Automatic tax filing.** Federal and state payroll taxes calculated, filed, and deposited automatically across all 50 states. This is now table stakes — your product must match it.

**Unlimited payroll runs.** No extra charge for off-cycle or bonus runs. Important for construction where pay schedules can be irregular (project completion bonuses, per diem adjustments).

**Embedded financial products.** Gusto is expanding into workers' comp (through AP Integro), 401(k) administration (Guideline acquisition), and even embedded credit (Payroll Bridge). They're becoming the financial operating system for SMBs — not just payroll.

They're going broader, not deeper into verticals.

### Gusto vs. QuickBooks: Different Threats, Different Lessons

| Dimension | QuickBooks Payroll | Gusto |
|---|---|---|
| **Threat type** | Incumbent inertia | UX benchmark |
| **What they own** | The accounting relationship | The simplicity expectation |
| **Construction depth** | Shallow (workarounds exist) | None (no workarounds) |
| **Switching trigger** | First complex payroll need | Realization it's not built for construction at all |
| **Your pitch against them** | "Your accounting tool now does real construction payroll" | "Gusto-simple, but built for how you actually pay crews" |
| **Strategic risk** | Could add construction features (large engineering org) | Low — going horizontal, not vertical |

### Strategic Implications for Your Product

**1. Study Gusto's interaction design, then exceed it for your domain.**

Gusto's payroll run flow, onboarding experience, and dashboard clarity are the gold standard for SMB payroll UX. Your product should feel *at least* this polished. The difference: where Gusto's simplicity comes from *omitting* complexity (no union fringes, no prevailing wage), yours comes from *absorbing* it. Same simple surface, far more sophisticated engine underneath.

**2. Gusto users are a prime acquisition segment.**

There's a natural lifecycle: a small contractor starts with Gusto because it's easy. They grow, take a government project, hire union workers, or expand to multiple states. Suddenly Gusto can't keep up. That moment — when the contractor realizes Gusto doesn't speak construction — is your entry point. Your onboarding should include a "migrating from Gusto" path that imports their employee data and makes the transition seamless.

**3. Don't compete on Gusto's expanding surface area.**

Gusto is adding 401(k), embedded credit, and global payroll. Don't chase those features. Your moat is construction-specific payroll depth inside an existing finance platform. Let Gusto win on breadth; you win on the specific, high-pain-point calculations that their architecture can't support.

**4. Borrow their trust-building patterns.**

Gusto builds trust through transparency: showing exactly what taxes were filed, when, and for how much. Your "Complexity Abstraction" needs the same. Don't just hide the complexity — show the *result* of the complexity in plain language. Something like: "Davis-Bacon rates applied ✓ — John earned $42.50/hr base + $18.75 fringe on the Highway 9 project this week." The user doesn't configure it, but they can see it's right.

**5. Price relative to Gusto Plus, not Gusto Simple.**

Your target user needs multi-state support at minimum, which puts Gusto at ~$195–$284/month for a 15-person crew. That's your comparison point. If your payroll feature adds $50–100/month over what they'd pay Gusto, and eliminates the need for a payroll specialist or external service for compliance, the ROI story writes itself.

---

### The Bottom Line

Gusto is the "beautiful but shallow" competitor. They've proven that SMB payroll can be delightful — that's valuable validation for your approach. But they've also proven, through their roadmap choices, that they're not coming for construction. Your opportunity is to build the product that construction contractors *wish* Gusto was: the same intuitive experience, but with the engine to handle the real complexity of how they pay their people.
